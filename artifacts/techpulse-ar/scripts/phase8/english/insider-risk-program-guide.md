Detecting suspicious insider activity is one thing; having an agreed, fair way to act on it is another. An insider risk programme is the organisational wrapper: who's involved, what's monitored, how cases are handled, and how the rights of employees are protected. Without it, security tools generate alerts nobody is allowed to act on, or worse, people act on them badly.

## Governance first

- **Sponsor**: a senior leader who owns the programme.
- **Working group**: security, HR, legal, privacy/data protection, and representatives of key business units.
- **Charter and policy**: the programme's purpose, scope, what data is used, who can see it, and how employees are informed.
- **Legal review** for employment and privacy law in every country you operate in.

## Know what you're protecting

List the "crown jewels": customer databases, source code, financial systems, designs, pricing. Identify who has access and through which channels data could leave. This keeps monitoring focused instead of watching everyone for everything.

## Risk indicators

Combine technical and organisational signals, handled with care:

- **Technical**: large downloads or syncs, access outside normal role or hours, uploads to personal cloud, USB copying, attempts to disable controls; see [insider threat detection](/article/insider-threat-detection-basics).
- **Organisational** (from HR, under strict rules): resignation or notice period, disciplinary processes, role changes.

A departing employee downloading the customer list is a stronger signal than either fact alone.

## Preventive controls

1. [Least privilege](/article/least-privilege-principle-practical) and regular access reviews.
2. **Joiner-mover-leaver** automation tied to HR records, so access changes on time.
3. **Offboarding**: account disablement on the last day, device return, token and shared-credential revocation.
4. [DLP](/article/data-loss-prevention-dlp-guide) and USB controls for the most sensitive data.
5. [Dual control](/article/dual-control-critical-operations) for critical and financial operations.

## Handling cases

- Documented triage: most alerts are explained by normal work.
- Escalation to HR and legal before any interview or action.
- Need-to-know access to case data, and an audit trail of who viewed it.
- Consistent treatment regardless of seniority.
- Evidence preserved properly in case of legal proceedings.

## Culture and transparency

Tell staff the programme exists, what it covers and why. Most insider incidents are mistakes, and a programme seen as fair encourages people to report problems, including their own errors.

## Measuring it

Track time to revoke access for leavers, the number of excessive-access findings fixed, DLP incidents by severity, and cases resolved. Review the programme yearly.

## FAQ

**Is this only for large companies?**
Small organisations can apply the core: clear offboarding, least privilege, a simple policy, and an agreed route to HR.
