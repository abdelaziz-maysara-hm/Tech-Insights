An incident response plan nobody has practised usually fails on simple things: no one knows who decides, the contact list is out of date, or the backup admin is the only person who knows the password. A tabletop exercise finds those gaps in a meeting room instead of during a real attack.

## What a tabletop is

A facilitated discussion where participants walk through a realistic scenario and explain what they would do at each step. No systems are touched. It takes two to four hours and needs little more than a room and a well-prepared facilitator.

## Planning

1. **Set objectives**: for example, "test decision-making for paying a ransom" or "check that IT and communications coordinate".
2. **Pick a realistic scenario** based on your actual risks: ransomware, business email compromise, a cloud data leak, an insider.
3. **Invite the right people**: IT and security, plus management, legal, communications, HR and business owners. Many of the hardest decisions aren't technical.
4. **Write injects**: new information released over time ("The attackers have posted a sample of customer data on their leak site", "A journalist calls").
5. **Brief participants**: it's a no-blame exercise; gaps are the point.

## Running it

- The facilitator presents the scenario and injects, and asks open questions: who does what, using which information, how do you communicate?
- A note-taker records decisions, assumptions and gaps.
- Keep momentum. If the group gets stuck, note it and move on.
- Test assumptions: "How would you contact staff if email is down?" "Where is the offline copy of this plan?"

## After the exercise

- **Hot wash** right away: what went well, what didn't.
- **Written report** with findings, owners and deadlines.
- **Update the plan**, contact lists and runbooks.
- **Repeat** at least yearly, with a different scenario, and include new staff.

A first scenario many teams use is the first hour of a ransomware attack; our [ransomware first-hour playbook](/article/ransomware-first-hour-response) can serve as the answer key.

## FAQ

**Do we need an outside facilitator?**
Not necessarily, but an outsider asks harder questions and lets the internal team participate fully.

**Where can we find ready-made scenarios?**
CISA publishes free Tabletop Exercise Packages (CTEPs) for many scenarios.
