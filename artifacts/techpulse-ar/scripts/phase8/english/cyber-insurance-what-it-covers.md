Cyber insurance has gone from a niche product to something boards and customers ask about. It can pay for a lot after an incident, but only under the policy's terms, and insurers now expect evidence of solid security before they offer cover.

## What policies typically cover

**First-party (your own losses):**
- Incident response: forensics, legal advice, breach coaching.
- Data restoration and system rebuild costs.
- Business interruption: lost income during downtime, after a waiting period.
- Notification costs, credit monitoring for affected people.
- Extortion and ransom-related costs, where legally permitted and depending on the policy.

**Third-party (claims against you):**
- Liability from customers or partners affected by a breach.
- Regulatory defence costs and, where insurable by law, fines.
- Media liability.

## Common exclusions and limits

- **War and state-sponsored attack** exclusions, whose wording has been tightened in recent years.
- **Unpatched known vulnerabilities** or controls you declared but didn't have.
- **Prior incidents** known before the policy started.
- **Social engineering / funds transfer fraud**, often with low sub-limits unless specifically added.
- **Betterment**: upgrades beyond restoring what you had.

## What underwriters look for

Expect questionnaires and sometimes external scans covering:

- MFA on email, remote access and privileged accounts.
- EDR on endpoints and servers.
- Backups that are offline or immutable and tested; see [immutable backups](/article/immutable-backups-ransomware-defense).
- Patch management, especially internet-facing systems.
- Privileged access management and [least privilege](/article/least-privilege-principle-practical).
- Incident response plan and training.

Answer accurately. Misstatements on the application can void a claim.

## Preparing for a claim

- Know the insurer's **notification deadline and hotline**, and keep them in your offline incident plan.
- Many policies require using **panel vendors** for forensics and legal; engaging others first may not be reimbursed.
- **Preserve evidence** and keep an incident log; see [ransomware first-hour response](/article/ransomware-first-hour-response).

## FAQ

**Does insurance replace security investment?**
No. It transfers some financial risk; it doesn't prevent downtime, reputational damage or regulatory scrutiny.
