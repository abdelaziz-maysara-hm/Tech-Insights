---
title: Running Phishing Simulations That Change Behaviour, Not Just Scores
excerpt: How to design an ethical, useful phishing simulation programme: baselines, realistic scenarios, instant teachable moments, measuring reports instead of clicks, and what to avoid.
---

Phishing simulations can build real reflexes, or they can teach staff that IT is out to trick them. The difference is in design: the goal is more people **reporting** suspicious messages quickly, not catching people out.

## Set it up right

- **Get leadership and HR on board** and agree that results are for learning, not discipline.
- **Allow-list the simulation platform** in your mail filters so tests reach inboxes.
- **Run a baseline** campaign before training to see where you start.

## Design realistic scenarios

- Mirror real threats your organisation sees: fake invoices, shared-document notifications, MFA or password-expiry warnings, delivery notices.
- Vary difficulty, from obvious to subtle, and include QR-code and attachment lures as well as links.
- **Avoid cruel lures**: fake bonuses, pay cuts, health scares or layoffs damage trust and have caused real backlash.

## Teach in the moment

When someone clicks, show a short page explaining the clues they missed. Thirty seconds right after the mistake works better than an hour of annual training.

## Measure what matters

| Metric | Why |
|---|---|
| Report rate | People actively spotting and reporting |
| Time to first report | How fast the SOC would hear about a real campaign |
| Click and credential-entry rates | Useful, but secondary |
| Repeat clickers | Who needs extra support, not punishment |

A rising report rate is the clearest sign the programme works. More on this in [security awareness metrics](/article/security-awareness-training-metrics).

## Cadence

Monthly or every other month, varied scenarios, randomised timing so colleagues don't warn each other. Pair simulations with short training on [spotting phishing](/article/phishing-spot-before-click).

## Close the loop

Make the report button easy, thank reporters (including for simulations), and act on real reports fast. If reporting leads nowhere, people stop doing it.

## FAQ

**Should executives be included?**
Yes. They're prime targets for whaling and business email compromise.

**Can simulations replace technical controls?**
No. Some phishing will always succeed; filtering, MFA and endpoint protection limit the damage.
